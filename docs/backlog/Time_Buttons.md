# Idea — Time buttons: one tap tells Pip *when*

**Status:** Never decided. Founder idea, 2026-09-25 ("a killer idea"). Not
work. Comes after phase 021 (in git history) and `022_Whose_And_How_Many.md`,
which build the machinery it would use. Name is a placeholder.

## The idea

Past tense is her meaning, not grammar. *I go to sleep* and *I went to
sleep* are different messages, and no data can tell which one she means.
CHILDES confirms it: past-tense verbs appear in about 4% of children's
sentences, and only 11% of those sentences contain any time word. So we
can't guess past tense from the other words. She has to tell us, and it
should take one tap.

A **time button**, for example a clock rewinding. One tap switches Grammar
help for this sentence into the past:

- **before she taps the verb:** after the time button, *I* → the *go* cell
  reads **went**, *eat* reads **ate**;
- **or after:** she built *I go to sleep*, taps the time button, and the
  sentence becomes **I went to sleep**.

A second button, a clock going forward, could do the future: *I go to
sleep* → **I am going to sleep**. The past is easier and comes first.

## Why it fits Pip

- It is a meaning word she chooses, like *two* or *he*, so it changes
  nothing she didn't ask for. Rewriting several words in her sentence breaks
  021's "only the word right before" rule, but that rule exists to stop
  guesses. This isn't a guess: she said *when*.
- SLPs teach *before / now / later* early, with visual schedules. One clear
  button fits how the concept is taught.
- Competitors make the child find *went* in a list of forms for every verb.
  Here it's one tap for the whole sentence.
- Once her forms are logged (021), her own habits show up: if she tells
  *I went…* stories every day at pickup time, the bar can offer the time
  button, or *went*, at that time. Individual first.

## What the data can decide (no rules)

- **Which verb changes.** In *I want to go*, only *want* becomes *wanted*;
  *go* stays, because it follows *to*. Past-tense sentences in CHILDES show
  this per position, the same way 021's form table does.
- **Past spellings,** from the data: *went, ate, had, was / were, didn't,
  could*.
- **The future's shape.** Children mostly say *going to* (*gonna*), less
  often *will*. The data says which, per verb.

## Open questions (for when it's picked up)

1. **Where it lives:** a new button next to Speak, a home-board cell, or
   replacing a core word. The Time group already has *yesterday, before,
   today, tomorrow, later, now, after*. Should tapping those trigger the
   same switch?
2. **Does it speak?** A rewind button could stay silent (it only changes
   the sentence), or say *before*. A silent marker keeps the sentence
   natural (*I went to sleep*, not *before I went to sleep*).
3. **The future adds words** (*am going to*), where the past only changes
   forms. That is new: Grammar help has never inserted a word before.
4. **Undo:** tapping it again returns the sentence to present.
5. **Clips:** every past form of every verb (about 100) and the future
   helpers.

## Proof, when it's built

Held-out CHILDES past-tense sentences: turn their verbs into present forms,
apply the time button, and count how often the result matches what the
child actually said. Plus founder-reviewed example rows (*I go to sleep* →
*I went to sleep*; *I want to go* → *I wanted to go*; *he is sad* → *he was
sad*).

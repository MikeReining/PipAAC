## Founder intent

Every learner is different. Pip ships strong defaults, and the parent or SLP
can change them. Consistency across upgrades is a goal, not dogma.

1. **No one-size law.** Presenting a motor-plan rule as settled science, or
   as the thing that makes a product "the standard", is not supported by
   evidence. Clinical practice starts from the individual (feature matching).
2. **Adults may move core words.** The app and prediction still never move a
   cell. A parent or SLP may, for their own child. This reverses the
   2026-09-22 rule that core indices are immutable to everyone.
3. **Graduate without starting over.** Moving up a density should not reset
   the child. Some movement is acceptable — children learn ABC order, then
   QWERTY; single words move as they become phrases. Change only when an
   adult chooses to, and not while the child's development is unchanged.
4. **`grid90` may move words.** Users who reach it already show strong motor
   and cognitive skill and can absorb a planned move. Left-to-right sentence
   order and Fitzgerald color still hold.
5. **The smallest board is always the clinician's pick,** but Pip leads with
   an opinionated 15-cell default, researched, and different people need
   different ones (a stroke survivor may never upgrade; the goal is to
   communicate now).

## Product value

- Families with CP, Rett, tremor, CVI, or an acquired condition get a board
  that fits the person instead of a fixed product shape.
- Upgrades are planned and visible, not a silent reset.
- The adult keeps authority over the child's board.

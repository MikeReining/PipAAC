Who gets the 15 new buttons

Same selection rule as the 60-button board (Core_Grid_Membership §2), run with 15 open seats:

  • sad: rule 0 (reporting that something is wrong). Every board should be able to say it.
  • mom · dad: rule 3. Core 15 has no button for calling a person; he · she would only repeat what I · you already do. An adult can put the family's own person here.
  • my · have: children say them about twice as often as put and far more than good (CHILDES), and my is on the toddler core list (Banajee). They replace put · good from the first draft.
  • get · do · in · up · it · on · here · look · that · can: Universal Core words that are also frequent in children's speech.
  • Left off on purpose: the · is · a are very frequent, but children at the one- and two-word stage leave them out, and they mean nothing tapped alone. need is rare in children's speech (a fifth as often as have), and want already asks for things.

Where they sit

Every word was placed as close as possible to where it sits on the 60-button board (6 × 5 has no exact match). This draft is within 3% of the best possible fit for these words, with two exceptions:

  • hurt stays in the bottom-right corner, where it is on both 15 and 60.
  • sad · help · hurt sit together in the bottom right: one corner for "something is wrong".

The colors keep the 60-button left-to-right order: people, actions, little words, describing, questions, safety.

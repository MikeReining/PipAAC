Money

Is Pip really free? What's the catch?

Free means free. Your child taps a word and Pip says it, for as long as they need it. You also get 20 words of your own, one helper, backup, and Spotlight. Every new board starts with a free week of everything, including natural sentences and the sentence buttons. After that, keeping those costs $49 once. That's the whole catch.

What does $49 get me?

A natural voice that reads the whole sentence, the sentence buttons, feeling faces, a choice of voices, a voice for words you type, the full progress dashboard and report, unlimited words of your own, every helper on the team, every device, and 300 pictures drawn in Pip's style. Once. Forever.

Will you ever make it a subscription?

No. A voice is not rented. Pip Lifetime is paid once, so there is nothing to renew or cancel.

Is it $49 per child or per family?

Per child: the person who speaks. Helpers never buy their own license.

I'm an SLP. Do I pay?

Never.

Yes: license codes, 50% off 10 or more, purchase orders welcome.

Making it yours

Can I really change anything?

Any word in any spot. Your photos, your names, your recordings. Show or hide groups. Start with 15 buttons or 90.

Won't changing things confuse my child?

Nothing changes unless you change it. Pip never moves, hides, or reshuffles words on its own, and when you do move one, Pip shows you first.

Will the buttons move around?

Never on their own. Suggestions live in a strip at the top; the board stays put.

What if my child outgrows the board?

Grow from 15 to 30, 60, or 90 words without starting over.

Can I record my own voice for a word?

Yes, for any word or name. Open the word (Settings, Words, All words) and tap Record your voice.

Getting started

What do I need?

An iPad, or any device with a modern browser. Tap Create your child's board. That's it.

Do I need an account to start?

No. Build the board first. When you've added your own people, save it with your email so it's backed up.

Is Pip for teens and adults?

Yes. Choose "a teen or adult" when you start, and you can choose words-only buttons.

Can I bring my board over from another app?

Pip is built differently, so you'll set up fresh, but it's quick, and many families run Pip alongside their old app while they switch.

Is this normal?

Answers from AAC research and speech-language practice. Every child is different: your SLP knows yours best.

They press the same button over and over, or tap at random. Is that OK?

Yes, and it's a good sign. It's called AAC babbling. Like a baby saying "ba-ba-ba", they're learning where each word lives and what it sounds like.

Answer as if they meant it ("More? You want more!"), and now and then show the next word ("more… juice"). Please don't turn the sound off or put Pip away. That's like covering a talking child's mouth.

They won't use Pip yet. Are we doing something wrong?

No. That's expected at first. Children hear thousands of words before they say one, and AAC is learned the same way: by watching you use it.

Point to words on their board as you talk ("we GO outside"), without asking them to copy you. Keep Pip in reach all day. Weeks or months before the first words of their own is normal.

How do I teach them to use it?

Show, don't test. This is called modeling. Point to the words on their board while you say them, in real moments: snack, bath, play, the car.

Use one more word than they do. If they say "go", you show "go outside". Then wait: count to five in your head and give them a turn. Don't make them repeat a word, or tap it to get something.

Spotlight lights up the words you're teaching, so they're easy to find for both of you.

Will Pip stop them from learning to talk?

No. Decades of research show AAC doesn't hold back speech, and many children say more out loud once they have a reliable way to be understood.

If they start saying words, celebrate it, and keep Pip there for when speech is hard.

Are they ready for AAC? Are they too young?

There's nothing to get ready for. Speech-language experts agree there are no prerequisites for AAC: not age, not attention span, not understanding pictures. Children learn it by using it, and toddlers can start too.

They say silly or rude words, or "no" all the time.

That's communication, and it's a big step: they've found out their words make things happen.

Answer like you would a talking child. Laugh at the joke, or say "You're mad. You can say stop." Saying no matters too. Never take Pip away as a punishment. It's their voice.

Using Pip

Can my child say things with feeling?

Yes: happy, sad, or angry, for any sentence. Turn on Feeling faces in Settings, Talking. The faces sit at the end of the Smart bar; tap one and the sentence is said with that feeling.

Does it work without Wi-Fi?

Yes. Tapping a word and hearing it works with no internet. The natural sentence voice and the sentence buttons need the internet.

Helpers and devices

How do grandparents and teachers join?

Invite them as helpers on their own phones or laptops: Settings, Team & devices, Invite. Teachers, therapists and grandparents then see and edit the same board.

Backup, privacy and trust

What if the iPad breaks?

Open Pip on any device and scan your child's card. Everything comes back.

Does Pip listen to us?

No. The microphone is only used when you choose to record a word.

Does Pip replace speech therapy?

No. It makes therapy and home work together.
